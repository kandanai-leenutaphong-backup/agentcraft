## Incident review

This reviewable operating incident1 report is prepared in accordance with clause 4.8.15(c) of the National Electricity Rules
(NER). It has been prepared using information provided by AusNet Services2, EnergyAustralia3 and from AEMO systems.

<tr>
<td>Incident details</td>
<td>This report relates to a reviewable operating incident4 that occurred at 1334 hrs on 16 October 2025 in Victoria. The incident involved the trip of the Yallourn 'W' Power Station (YWPS) unit 3 and unit 4. Each unit trip involved the disconnection of its associated 220/20 kilovolt (kV) transformer.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Incident classification</td>
<td>Protection/control system - cooling water isolating valve actuator control circuit was damaged, likely due to long-term flooding in the valve pit, which allowed the inadvertent remote electrical operation of the valve while the valve actuator was in a local manual operation mode.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Generation impact</td>
<td>A total of 597 megawatts (MW) of generation was lost as a result of this incident.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Customer load impact</td>
<td>No load was lost as a result of this incident.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Pre-incident conditions</td>
<td>Prior to the incident, YWPS unit 3 was generating 299 MW and unit 4 was generating 298 MW.<br>There were sufficient units online in Victoria for system strength with Loy Yang A (LYA) Power Station (PS) unit 1, LYA PS unit 3, Loy Yang B (LYB) PS unit 2, YWPS unit 1, YWPS unit 3 and YWPS unit 4 online. Dartmouth PS and Newport PS were bid unavailable, which reduced the available system strength combinations.<br>EnergyAustralia was undertaking commissioning of a new alternating current (AC) distribution supply board to replace the existing distribution board which supplies the cooling system common to YWPS unit 3 and unit 4. The YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 common cooling system is separate to that of YWPS unit 1 and unit 2.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Incident key events</td>
<td>On 16 October 2025 the following events occurred:<br>1. At 1334.29 hrs, YWPS unit 4 tripped immediately following the electrical operation of the valve actuator.<br>2. At 1334.32 hrs, YWPS unit 3 tripped (see Figure 1).<br>3. At 1350 hrs, AEMO directed Jeeralang units A2, A3 and B3 to synchronise and follow targets to manage system strength.<br>- Jeeralang unit A2 was synchronised to the network at 1401 hrs.<br>- Jeeralang unit A3 was synchronised to the network at 1401 hrs.<br>- Jeeralang unit B3 was synchronised to the network at 1405 hrs.<br>4. At 1627 hrs, YWPS unit 3 was resynchronised to the network.<br>5. At 1755 hrs, AEMO cancelled the directions to Jeeralang units A2, A3 and B3 from 1800 hrs on 16 October 2025.<br>On 17 October 2025, at 0152 hrs, YWPS unit 4 was resynchronised to the network.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Incident cause</td>
<td>Post-incident investigation by EnergyAustralia has concluded that the trip of YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 was caused by a loss of cooling water flow to both units. This occurred during commissioning works to replace the cooling system power supply distribution board. A cooling water isolating valve actuator operated unexpectedly, resulting in the valve closing and interrupting cooling water supply common to both units.<br>The isolating water valve actuator control circuit, likely damaged due to long-term flooding in the pit, allowed an inadvertent remote electrical operation from the new distribution board while the valve actuator was in a local manual operation mode (electrically isolated).</td>
</tr>
</table>

<td>The loss of cooling water flow resulted in the loss of condensation capability and the rapid deterioration of condenser vacuum levels. Under these conditions, generator protection operated as expected, resulting in the trip of YWPS unit 3 and unit 4.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Power system response (facilities and services)</td>
<td>All protection and circuit breaker (CB) operations were as expected during this incident.<br>There was no other material impact on the broader power system, load or generation.<br>AEMO has reviewed YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 performance and has not identified any compliance issues.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Rectification</td>
<td>Immediately following the incident, EnergyAustralia disconnected the electrical supply cables to the affected valve actuator, with the disconnection implemented as a permanent measure to prevent any further electrical operation of the valve. EnergyAustralia also permanently disconnected the electrical supply cables to other similar cooling water isolating valve actuators in the affected cooling water system. EnergyAustralia intends to operate these cooling system water valves in manual operation mode from now on and only plans to operate these valves during maintenance.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Power system security</td>
<td>Following this non-credible contingency, the combination of generating units online in Victoria was below the minimum combination threshold as specified in AEMO's Victorian system strength limit advice. Based on the availability of fast-start units at the time, and accounting for the cost of direction(s), AEMO determined that three Jeeralang A or B units were required online to manage system strength in Victoria.<br>AEMO directed Jeeralang units A2, A3 and B3 online to meet one of the secure unit combinations specified in AEMO's Victorian system strength limit advice. Jeeralang units A2 and A3 were synchronised 27 minutes after the non-credible contingency, and Jeeralang unit B3 was synchronised 31 minutes after the non-credible contingency.<br>Prior to the incident, AEMO was aware that, to simplify the unit combination definitions for secure system strength levels, the published Victorian system strength limit advice treats all LYA and LYB units equivalently. However, there are differences in individual LYA and LYB units' system strength contributions due to variations in their machine characteristics, particularly the subtransient reactance5 parameter. To account for this and ensure sufficient system strength is always available, in the definition of the limit advice, it is conservatively assumed that the specific Loy Yang units offering the lowest system strength contribution are dispatched first. AEMO's post-incident investigation concluded that Victoria's minimum fault level requirements were met throughout this incident6. Electromagnetic transient (EMT) studies further confirmed that the power system was operating in a secure state within 30 minutes following the non-credible trip of YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 and post-synchronisation of Jeeralang units A2 and A3. This was due to the favourable system strength contribution from LYA and LYB units which were online at the time.<br>AEMO's post-incident power system security assessment further confirmed for this event that if a different combination of three LYA or LYB units were dispatched, the available fault level may have been insufficient to ensure all Victorian fault level nodes operated above their minimum requirement. The Victorian system strength limit advice is undergoing review such that additional system strength combinations can be made available for operational use. A further review is planned to identify the best approach to consider unit-specific factors while maintaining operational clarity.<br>Following the trip of YWPS unit 3 and unit 4, the mainland frequency reached a minimum of 49.91 hertz (Hz), remaining within the normal operating frequency band (NOFB). The Frequency Operating Standard (FOS)7 was met for this incident.<br>AEMO will publish a report in accordance with NER 4.8.9(f) and 3.13.6A(a) in relation to the directions to Jeeralang units A2, A3 and B3.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Reclassification</td>
<td>AEMO assessed whether to reclassify this incident as a credible contingency event8.<br>Following the incident, on 17 October 2025, EnergyAustralia advised AEMO that the trip of YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 was caused by a cooling system water valve electrical issue common to both units, the valve actuator power supply had been isolated, and the event was unlikely to re-occur. Based on this advice, AEMO was satisfied that another occurrence of this event was unlikely and did not re-classify it as a credible contingency event. AEMO appropriately applied the reclassification criteria and determined they were not met based on the information available to AEMO at the time.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Market information</td>
<td>AEMO issued market notices (MNs) to inform the market of the events. These MNs are summarised in Table 2. All MNs for this incident were issued in accordance with NER requirements.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Conclusions</td>
<td>AEMO has concluded that:<br>1. On 16 October 2025, YWPS unit 3 and unit 4 tripped due to the loss of cooling system water flow to both units during commissioning works to replace the cooling system power supply distribution board. The cooling water isolating valve actuator control circuit, damaged likely due to long-term flooding in the pit, allowed the inadvertent remote electrical operation of the valve while the actuator was in local manual operation mode.<br>2. EnergyAustralia permanently disconnected the electrical supply cables to the affected cooling water isolating valve actuator to prevent a recurrence and also disconnected the electrical supply cables to other similar cooling water isolating valve actuators in the cooling water system. These valves will now operate only in manual mode.</td>
</tr>
</table>
